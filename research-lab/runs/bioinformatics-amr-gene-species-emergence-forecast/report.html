For first appearances of antibiotic-resistance genes in new species, sampling effort beats a trained model at the top of the list; for confirmations, the model ranks about four times better overall

NCBI Pathogen Detection, 106 organism groups: 1.62 million isolates with uniform AMRFinderPlus calls, 264 species, 4,417 gene symbols. Candidate (gene, species) pairs not yet established by the end of 2022 were ranked for appearance in 2023–2025. Two rankings were compared: a gradient-boosted model trained on the same task one period earlier, and a sampling-effort null that knows only each gene's frequency and how many isolates of each species were sequenced in the forecast window. Isolates are dated by collection, so this is a retrospective test, not a real-time forecast. The plan was committed before any data were downloaded. This version follows an independent review and a confirmation pass that both returned "fix first" (deviations D3 and D3b), and a second confirmation pass (publish with edits; D3c).

Verdict: H1 Refuted by the preregistered rule; H2 Inconclusive.

  • For genuine first appearances the effort null wins at the top of the list. Under the preregistered variant, in which one isolate is enough to count as a first appearance:
    • the null puts about 15 true first appearances in its top 100 (precision 0.150);
    • the model puts none at nine of ten seeds;
    • over the whole list, the model's average precision is 1.4–2.4 times the null's.
  • H1 (the model's precision@100 is at least twice the null's): Refuted.
    • At the preregistered seed the model scores 0.00 against the null's 0.070 (ratio 0, 95% interval 0 to 0.50).
    • That fit is the worst of ten. At the other nine seeds the ratio is 1.4–2.7, and five clear the 2× bar.
  • For confirmations the model is better overall. Under the primary two-BioProject rule, its average precision is 3.7–4.4 times the null's at every seed (0.075–0.090 against 0.021).
  • H2 (ESM-2 protein embeddings add signal): Inconclusive.
    • The paired difference in average precision is −0.005 (−0.013 to +0.001). The first draft mislabelled this Refuted.
    • Across seeds the embedding adds a little at seven of ten.
  • What the primary rule rewards. It needs two isolates from different BioProjects. Of its 847 emergences, 705 (83%) already had a carrier collected before 2023, but only 316 (37%) had one deposited, and so public, by then. The contrast between the two rules suggests that much of the model's skill is in ranking confirmations of pairs that already existed, often unpublished at the cutoff.
  • The scoring of the null was fixed twice.
    • Its probability saturates at 1.0 for thousands of candidates, and row order then decided the first draft's 0.08.
    • The first fix (log P of two or more) saturated at 0.0 in the same place and gave 0.033.
    • The second fix (log P of at most one, via logcdf) still underflowed for about 200 of the most heavily sequenced candidates, which tied them.
    • Computed in closed form, log P(≤1) = (n−1)·log1p(−f) + log1p((n−1)·f), the score stays finite. The null then has 988 distinct scores in its top 1,000 and precision@100 0.070 (D3c).

In plain terms

Genes that make bacteria resistant to antibiotics spread between species. We asked whether it is possible to forecast which gene will turn up in which species next, using 1.6 million sequenced isolates that one pipeline has annotated the same way. Any forecast has to beat a simple baseline that knows only how common each gene is and how many samples of each species are being sequenced.

For a gene's very first appearance in a species, it does not: the baseline puts about 15 real first appearances in its top 100 guesses, and the trained model almost never puts any. Where the model does better, about four times better over its whole list, is in predicting which gene–species pairs will be confirmed by a second, separate study. Many confirmed pairs had already been sampled before the cutoff, often not yet published.

Our first draft got several things wrong. It relied on the one model run, of ten, that did worst. It also scored the baseline in a way that let the order of rows in a file decide its result. An independent review caught both.

What was asked

The lead (bioinformatics-amr-gene-species-emergence-forecast) asked whether first appearances of AMR genes in new species can be forecast prospectively against an explicit sampling-effort null. - The uniform calls come from AMRFinderPlus and its Reference Gene Catalog (Feldgarden et al., 2021). - The feasibility claim comes from Ellabaan et al. (2021), who found dissemination partly predictable retrospectively. - The gap is a calendar-time test against an effort null.

How it was done

  • Corpus. All 106 bacterial organism groups' metadata tables. Kept: isolates with a collection year in 1990–2025, a resolved species binomial and an AMR call field; acquired genes only.
  • Pairs and candidates.
    • A pair is established in a year when two isolates from different BioProjects carry it.
    • Candidates at the 2022 cutoff are pairs not established by then, where the gene is established somewhere and the species has at least 20 isolates: 472,779 candidates, of which 847 emerged in 2023–2025.
  • The model. Histogram gradient boosting over 14 features in four families: sampling effort, co-occurrence, taxonomy, and ESM-2 embedding similarity. It is trained on the 2019 → 2020–2022 task, with hyperparameters chosen by gene-grouped cross-validation (D2). After the review it was refitted with ten seeds at those hyperparameters (D3).
  • The null. The probability that a gene at its pre-2023 frequency appears at least twice among the species' 2023–2025 isolates. It is computed in closed form as −log P(≤1) = −[(n−1)·log1p(−f) + log1p((n−1)·f)], which stays finite for every candidate (D3c).
  • Scoring. Precision@k is the exact expectation under random order within tied scores (D3). Intervals come from resampling candidates by gene, 2,000 times. The catalog-growth restriction (D1) and its sensitivity are reported.

  • The preregistered fit's top 100 is unusual. It is saturated (118 scores above 0.999), and 93 of its pairs are in two rarely sequenced species. No other seed does this.
  • The null ranks well but is badly calibrated. It has 988 distinct scores in its top 1,000: precision 0.070 at k = 100 and 0.060 at k = 500, and recall 0.059 at k = 1,000. But its top decile predicts 0.47 against an observed 0.011.
  • Literature check (the model's 20 highest-ranked true emergences): 15 of 20 are co-mentioned with their species in at least one Europe PMC record before 2023. Most appear in only one or two records, and a co-mention does not show the gene was found in that species (epmc.csv).

What this does and does not show

  • The model beats sampling effort at ranking confirmations. On average precision it does so at every seed and under every rule, by about four to five and a half times except for first appearances (1.4–2.4×). That is the robust finding.
  • The preregistered H1 is refuted only by a fragile statistic. Precision@100 on 847 positives among 472,779 candidates moves from 0.00 to 0.19 between seeds. A single fit should not have been the primary test; the review caught this, and the verdict is reported with that caveat rather than changed.
  • For genuine first appearances, sampling effort is the better forecaster at the top. The null's top 100 holds about 15; no model put one there at nine of ten seeds.
  • The embedding's role is unclear. It neither clearly helps nor clearly hurts. The first draft's claim that it "breaks the model" described one seed and is withdrawn.
  • Limits.
    • Retrospective, not real-time. 45% of isolates collected by 2022 were deposited after 2022, so the features and labels use data that were not public at the cutoff (D3).
    • Observation is not emergence. The effort null addresses sampling only in part.
    • The model is not the lead's. It is gradient-boosted trees over engineered features, not a temporal graph network.
    • Coverage. Pathogen Detection covers chosen, mainly clinical and foodborne, pathogens. Dates are by year; 599,000 isolates had no usable year and 722,000 no AMR call field.
    • Not run. The gene × country variant was not run (D3).
  • Not shown: that richer features (plasmid context, geography, host) would forecast first appearances; anything about clinical risk.
