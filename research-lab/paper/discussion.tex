\paragraph{What the protocol delivered.} The core commitment held in all studies but one: a public plan, committed
before outcome data were analysed. In the exception, the first study, the plan was committed together with its
results. The decision rules forced nulls to be reported as nulls. Only \VerdictSupported{} of \VerdictsDecided{}
decided verdicts support the preregistered hypothesis; the rest are refutations, unsupported or inconclusive. This is
far below the share of positive results in conventional literatures and below even the roughly 44\% reported for
registered reports in psychology \citep{scheel2021positive}. It is the opposite of the curated lists of discoveries
by which autonomous systems are usually presented.

Independent review was not a formality. Every review asked for changes, and \ReviewFixFirst{} of
\ReviewStudies{} asked for them before publication. Review changed \ReviewHeadlineChanged{} headline estimates and
\ReviewVerdictChanged{} verdicts, and the deviation logs record each change with a timestamp. In one study the
pre-review draft was never committed, so what review changed there cannot be reconstructed.

\paragraph{What did not work.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Uneven safeguards.} The strongest safeguard, keeping outcomes out of the agent's reach, was used in only
\InvBlindStrong{} of \StudiesTotal{} studies. In the rest, the plan's disclosure is the only protection, and it relies
on the agent's honesty about what it had seen.
\item \textbf{Missing power calculations.} Only one plan stated a power calculation or minimum detectable
effect, and one more did so partially. This plausibly explains why half the decided verdicts are \emph{Inconclusive}: the scouted effects were
rarely compared with what the data could detect before the work began. Kirgis et al.\ report the same weakness in
frontier agents \citep{kirgis2026}.
\item \textbf{Effort estimates.} Scouts estimated a median of \ScoutDaysMedian{} days per study, \ScoutDaysTotal{} days
in total. The \ReportsAfterFirst{} reports after the first appeared within \CalendarBulkDays{} days. Speed is not a
virtue in itself here. It reflects studies scoped to what an agent can do quickly with open data, and the same
fluency at engineering that Kirgis et al.\ observed alongside weak research judgement.
\item \textbf{The first study.} Its plan was committed together with its results. Its report nevertheless describes
itself as preregistered. The plan may well have been written first, but the public record cannot show it, and we have
added a correction to the report.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the audit itself teaches.} Naive automated tracing of report numbers to result files, which we and
others have recommended, is almost uninformative. Numbers from one study ``trace'' to another study's results
\NumChanceAll{} of the time, because the result files contain thousands of numbers. Tracing is informative only for
numbers with at least three significant figures, and only after correcting for chance matches. Tools that link claims
to evidence need to link each claim to a specific cell, as data-to-paper does \citep{ifargan2025datatopaper}, rather
than search for a matching value.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The agent audits itself.} The agent that ran the studies wrote this paper. Independent coders reduce,
but do not remove, the conflict of interest. All the coders and reviewers are models from a single vendor.
CrossAudit's finding that two vendors read the same rulebook differently \citep{crossaudit2026} suggests a
cross-vendor reviewer would catch different problems.
\item \textbf{Git timestamps are self-attested.} The agent controls the repository. Commits pushed to a public remote
are hard to rewrite unnoticed, but they are not an independent timestamp. A registry (OSF) or a trusted timestamping
service would be.
\item \textbf{No external check of conclusions.} No domain expert checked whether the conclusions are right. The audit
measures process, not truth.
\item \textbf{Small sample.} Nineteen studies from one lab, one agent family and one month are a case series, not an
estimate for autonomous research in general.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Changes to the protocol.} The audit and the literature point to eight changes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item timestamp each plan with an external registry as well as git;
\item require a minimum detectable effect, and an equivalence-type rule for \emph{Refuted}, in every plan;
\item keep outcomes out of the agent's reach whenever a design allows;
\item publish the reviewer's prompt and full review, and a ledger of every analysis run on outcome data;
\item link every number in a report to a specific result cell;
\item add a reviewer from another vendor for \emph{Supported} verdicts, and a blind re-analysis of each headline by
a second analyst agent given only the plan and the data \citep{gao2026nse,bertran2026many};
\item record scout estimates against outcomes and recalibrate the lead scoring;
\item state in every report which decisions were made by a human.
\end{enumerate}
