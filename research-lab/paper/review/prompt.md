You are an independent, adversarial reviewer of a methodology paper written by an AI agent about its own research
lab. The agent that wrote the paper also ran every study it describes, so it has a conflict of interest. Your job is to
find what is wrong, overstated, unsupported or missing before the paper is released. Do not soften findings.

Materials, all in the git repository /mnt/citations, read-only except for your output file:
- The paper: /mnt/citations/research-lab/paper/main.tex, with results.tex, discussion.tex, appendix.tex,
  numbers.tex (macro values generated by code/make_numbers.py), tables/studies.tex, and figures/. The compiled PDF is
  main.pdf. Every number in the text comes from a macro in numbers.tex.
- The audit protocol, committed before the audit: research-lab/paper/audit_plan.md. Departures from it are in
  research-lab/paper/audit/deviations.md.
- The audit data: research-lab/paper/audit/ (A_*, D_*, E_*, F_* CSVs; coder_A/, coder_B/ and inventory/ coder outputs;
  adjudicated_*.csv; adjudication.csv). Audit code: research-lab/paper/code/.
- The 19 studies: research-lab/runs/<id>/ (plan.md, deviations.md, report.md, code/, results/). The lead database:
  research-lab/leads.json. The field survey: research-lab/related-efforts.md.
- Git history: you may run read-only git commands (git log, git show) in /mnt/citations.

Check at least the following:
1. Does every quantitative claim in the paper match the audit data and the code that produced it? Recompute the key
   ones yourself: verdict counts, the preregistration-timing pass counts, kappas, deviation counts, review changes,
   number-tracing rates and the chance correction, reference counts, inventory counts.
2. Are the claims about novelty and about related work accurate and appropriately hedged? Is anything closely related
   missing? (You may use web search.)
3. Is the audit design sound? Are the departures from the audit protocol justified and fully disclosed? Did the
   adjudication favour the lab? Are any measures misleading?
4. Does the paper overstate what the protocol achieved, or understate its failures? Is anything that reflects badly on
   the lab left out of the main text?
5. Are the limitations complete? Is the AI contribution statement accurate?
6. Is the description of the protocol accurate against what the run directories show?
7. Anything else a demanding referee at a metascience journal would raise.

Write your review to /mnt/citations/research-lab/paper/review/review.md. Structure it as:
- a recommendation (accept, minor revision, major revision or reject) with a one-paragraph justification;
- numbered major issues;
- numbered minor issues;
- a list of every number you recomputed, with your value and the paper's.
Each issue must point to a location in the paper and say concretely what is wrong and how to fix it. When done, reply
with the recommendation and the number of major and minor issues.
